Geometric Projection Particle Filtering under Model
Uncertainty
Surya Ratna Prakash D Soumyendu Raha
Abstract—Nonlinear state estimation under structural model
uncertainty remains a central challenge in autonomous Guidance,
Navigation, and Control (GNC) systems. Classical estimators
propagate states using assumed dynamics and incorporate mea-
surements through posterior correction, which under mismatch
leads to biased innovations, estimator inconsistency, and particle
degeneracy due to proposal–likelihood mismatch.
This paper presents the Geometric Projection Particle Filter
(GPF), a measurement-informed framework in which observation
geometry directly influences state propagation. The method
projects nominal drift dynamics onto the measurement-consistent
subspace, enforcing local compatibility between dynamics and ob-
servations. This yields a geometry-consistent proposal process that
reduces effective mismatch prior to weighting while preserving
the Bayesian posterior via a change-of-measure formulation.
A continuous-time formulation is developed, introducing a
geometric co-state variable that characterizes mismatch-induced
inconsistency and has zero conditional mean under correct
modeling. It is shown that filtering error is governed by the
component of mismatch orthogonal to the measurement-consistent
subspace, and particle consistency is established with convergence
at the standard Monte Carlo rate.
The approach is evaluated on lunar descent navigation scenarios
with partial observability and persistent mismatch. Results
demonstrate significant reduction in particle degeneracy, sustained
effective sample size, and bounded estimation error in regimes
where standard particle filters diverge, achieving up to an order-
of-magnitude improvement in accuracy.
These findings show that enforcing geometric compatibility dur-
ing propagation provides a principled mechanism for improving
robustness and consistency in nonlinear particle filtering under
model uncertainty.
Keywords: nonlinear filtering; particle filtering; geometric
projection; change of measure; model uncertainty; estimator
integrity; partial observability; autonomous navigation.
I. INTRODUCTION
Reliable nonlinear state estimation is essential in safety-
critical Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC) systems,
including spacecraft, planetary landers, and autonomous vehi-
cles. In such settings, estimators must operate under persistent
structural model uncertainty, nonlinear sensing, and partial or
time-varying observability. Maintaining statistical consistency
of the estimated state distribution under these conditions
remains a central challenge.
Classical estimation architectures, including the Extended
Kalman Filter (EKF) and bootstrap Particle Filter (SPF), adopt a
two-stage structure in which state propagation uses an assumed
dynamical model, followed by measurement-based posterior

correction. While this separation is computationally convenient,
it introduces a fundamental limitation: under model mismatch,
the propagated state distribution is generally inconsistent with
the geometry induced by the observation model. In EKF-
type methods, this manifests as biased innovations and loss
of statistical consistency, whereas in particle filtering, the
mismatch is absorbed into the likelihood, leading to rapid
weight degeneracy and collapse of the effective sample size
[1]–[9].
A substantial body of work has addressed particle degen-
eracy and proposal mismatch within the sequential Monte
Carlo (SMC) framework. Foundational formulations based on
Feynman–Kac representations and interacting particle systems
are well established [10], [11], and fundamental limitations
in high-dimensional settings have been extensively analyzed
[12]–[15].
Numerous approaches have been proposed to mitigate
degeneracy, including optimal and auxiliary proposals [16],
[17], implicit sampling [18], [19], particle flow methods
[20], transport and ensemble-based approaches [21]–[23], and
feedback particle filters [24]. More recent developments include
score-based and diffusion-driven methods [25], as well as robust
and learning-based approaches for model uncertainty [26]–[28].
Despite these advances, however, existing methods primarily
operate at the level of global proposal design, ensemble
transport, or density evolution, and do not explicitly en-
force local measurement–dynamics compatibility at the level
of infinitesimal state evolution. As a result, under model
mismatch, inconsistency between dynamics and observations
is not corrected during propagation but accumulates in the
likelihood, inducing intrinsic proposal–likelihood mismatch.
This mechanism is a primary driver of particle degeneracy
and estimator degradation in high-uncertainty regimes such as
planetary descent and autonomous navigation [29].
Contribution and approach. To address proposal–likelihood
mismatch, we introduce the Geometric Projection Particle
Filter (GPF), a measurement-informed proposal that modifies
the drift of the assumed dynamics at the infinitesimal level.
The approach enforces first-order compatibility between state
evolution and the measurement map by projecting the nominal
drift onto the tangent space defined by the instantaneous
measurement Jacobian.
This yields a locally geometry-consistent proposal in which
particles evolve along measurement-compatible directions while
retaining stochastic excitation through the diffusion term. The
modification acts at the level of the proposal measure, and the
posterior under the physical measure is recovered via a standard

In contrast to transport-based, optimization-based, and

A central outcome is a geometric co-state variable that

measurement-informed proposal process for Bayesian filtering

IV. CONNECTION TO NONLINEAR FILTERING

measurement-informed proposal process for Bayesian state
estimation. In this section, we establish how this proposal can
be embedded within the continuous-time nonlinear filtering
framework via a change-of-measure construction.
The projected dynamics do not represent the posterior
filtering equation; instead, they define a guided sampling
mechanism. The posterior distribution is recovered through
likelihood-based reweighting under the physical measure.

during propagation, so that only the residual inconsistency
contributes to weight variability. This effectively transforms the
dominant failure mode from collapse to bounded dispersion in
typical regimes.
Remark 6 (Relation to Other Guided Proposals). Unlike
transport-based, implicit, or score-driven particle filtering meth-
ods that require solving global optimization or flow construction
problems, the GPF applies a closed-form local projection
independently to each particle. This yields a computationally
efficient, geometry-driven proposal mechanism suitable for
real-time applications.

Estimator integrity under model uncertainty is assessed using
diagnostic metrics that quantify the internal consistency of
the particle representation. In particular, effective sample size
(ESS) and the co-state magnitude ∥λt∥are used to characterize
particle degeneracy and model–measurement inconsistency,
respectively.
Large values of ESS(t) indicate well-distributed weights,
while small values reflect degeneracy and loss of posterior di-
versity. Sustained degradation of ESS(t) is therefore indicative
of proposal–likelihood mismatch.

I. Discussion of Results
The empirical results corroborate the theoretical framework.
In conventional particle filtering, model mismatch induces
weight dispersion, particle degeneracy, and subsequent error
growth. In contrast, the proposed GPF mitigates this mechanism
by enforcing measurement-consistent propagation, thereby
preserving particle diversity and maintaining low estimation
error.
Across all scenarios, degeneracy emerges as a primary driver
of instability. However, its coupling with error growth is signif-
icantly weakened under GPF, leading to improved robustness
and stability. These results demonstrate that modifying proposal
dynamics through geometric projection provides a principled
approach for addressing model mismatch in nonlinear filtering.
Extensions to higher-level autonomy and decision-making
frameworks are left for future work.
IX. CONCLUSION
This paper introduced the Geometric Projection Particle
Filter (GPF) for nonlinear state estimation under structural
model uncertainty and partial observability. The approach
embeds measurement geometry directly into state propagation,
enforcing local compatibility between dynamics and observa-
tions.
The main contributions are as follows:
• Geometry-consistent proposal dynamics: A projection-
based
propagation
that

the

into
measurement-consistent and orthogonal components, re-

Bayesian posterior via a change-of-measure formulation.
• Robustness under model mismatch: A characterization
showing that filtering error is governed by the component
of mismatch orthogonal to the measurement-consistent
subspace, with projection attenuating its impact.
• Particle consistency: Convergence of the empirical mea-
sure to the limiting distribution with standard Monte Carlo
rate.
• Co-state inconsistency: A geometric co-state variable
that quantifies mismatch-induced inconsistency and links
model mismatch to degeneracy and error growth.
• Empirical validation: Numerical results demonstrating
reduced degeneracy, improved effective sample size, and
bounded estimation error under model mismatch and
partial observability.
Overall, enforcing measurement consistency during propaga-
tion provides a principled mechanism for improving robustness
in nonlinear filtering by reducing effective mismatch prior to
weighting.
Future work will address degeneracy dynamics, integration
with control architectures, and extension to high-dimensional
estimation problems.
