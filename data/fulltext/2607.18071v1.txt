Hubbard-corrected density-functional theory (DFT+U ) is a popular tool for first-principles model-
ing of materials with localized d or f electrons, but its on-site corrections tend to over-localize charge
and break covalent bonds. Inter-site +V corrections were introduced to counter this and are now
widely used, but a formal justification has been lacking. Here we show that — to first order in V/U
— inter-site corrections are exactly equivalent to on-site DFT+U evaluated on a density-dependent
redefinition of the Hubbard projectors, hybridized with those of neighboring sites, providing insight
into the explicit mechanism by which V affects covalency.
If the projectors are held frozen, as
is common practice, the equivalence partially breaks down. Beyond reinterpreting the formalism,
these results sharpen the questions of how V should be computed and what the Hubbard subspaces
fundamentally are.

The derivation of DFT+U from the Hubbard model

them, that all electronic interactions are neglected ex-
cept for local Hartree-Fock terms within each Hubbard
site. Consequently, DFT+U does not model genuinely
strongly correlated chemistry; even so, it has in practice
been applied with remarkable success to many different
classes of materials (see Refs. [16–18], among many oth-
ers).
DFT+U can be placed on more rigorous and trans-
parent footing by framing it as a correction not of strong
correlations, but of self-interaction [19, 20]. This alter-
native interpretation rests on the observation that the
Hubbard correction of Eq. (1) reduces the spurious cur-
vature of the total energy with respect to the occupations
of each Hubbard subspace (more specifically, the eigen-

thus be seen as counteracting “many-body” SIE (with
the important caveat that correcting this local curvature

spect to total particle number; see Supp. Mat. S1 [21]).

This interpretation not only helps to explain the success
of DFT+U , but framing DFT+U as a correction to SIE
also provides us with a recipe to choose the Hubbard pa-
rameters {U I}: compute the erroneous curvature directly
from linear response DFT, and then set {U I} to match
the observed error (see Supp. Mat. S2). Since DFT+U
predictions depend strongly on the value of U, this un-
biased first-principles procedure is a prerequisite for the
theory to be predictive.
However, even with a recipe for computing Hubbard
parameters, the DFT+U method is not without its short-
comings — not least that the choice of the Hubbard
projectors themselves remains largely arbitrary, a point
to which we will return.
In addition, it has been ob-
served that DFT+U can over-localize charge and artifi-
cially break covalent bonds [22, 23]. This arises because
the Hubbard corrections shift the energy levels of the
projector orbitals, disrupting any hybridization (i.e. co-
valency) that might be occurring between these orbitals
and those of neighboring atoms (because effective hy-
bridization between orbitals requires them to have sim-
ilar energies) [24, 25]. This is not a corner case: away
from the most ionic compounds, metal–ligand hybridiza-
tion is the rule rather than the exception, and a purely
on-site correction cannot see it. In an effort to address
this, DFT+U has been extended to DFT+U +V :

Our reinterpretation of DFT+U +V as DFT+U ap-
plied to hybridized density-dependent projectors has con-
sequences for how V ought to be computed. If +V is re-
ally +U on the hybridized projectors, the self-consistent
way to parametrize it would be to enforce linearity of the
total energy in the occupations of these hybridized pro-
jectors, nII[ ˆS]. But that occupation responds through
inter-site channels — both the response of the inter-site
blocks nIJσ and the response to inter-site perturbations
— that the conventional response matrices do not contain
(see Supp. Mat. S3 for details). In other words, the equiv-
alence we have uncovered does not retroactively justify
computing V as the off-diagonal elements of the response
matrices used to compute U as is common practice [26].
This line of thought raises deeper questions: why might
we want to linearize the total energy with respect to
the occupation of the hybridized projectors, rather than
the original projectors — or any other choice? Is there
an “optimal” set of projectors for DFT+U ? A broad
family of DFT+U -inspired functionals — e.g. Koop-
mans functionals [39–41], the localized orbital scaling
correction [42, 43], Wannier-localized optimally-tuned
range-separated hybrid functionals [44], and Koopmans-
Wannier functionals [45] — corrects quasiparticle ener-
gies by imposing piecewise linearity of the total energy
with respect to localized orbitals (typically Wannier func-
tions or variants thereof [46, 47]), and the localized na-
ture of these orbitals is crucial to their success [40]. Why
correcting the curvature with respect to localized orbitals
should so effectively correct the energies of delocalized
quasiparticles deserves further investigation [48].

the frozen-projector regime used in practice this equiv-
alence is only partial — the spectrum and the total en-
ergy are reproduced by differently hybridized projectors
— but wherever the redefinition can be folded into frozen
projectors, an ordinary DFT+U calculation may stand
in for DFT+U +V — bringing the far broader ecosystem
of DFT+U implementations and property extensions
to bear — albeit only approximately, since no frozen-
projector functional reproduces the DFT+U +V poten-
tial exactly. The message to practitioners of DFT+U and
DFT+U +V is that the choice of projectors, the choice
of Hubbard parameters, and the choice of whether to
apply inter-site corrections are deeply intertwined and
should never be treated as independent degrees of free-
dom.
More deeply — with DFT+U +V now embed-
ded in community codes and automated high-throughput
workflows [49–53], increasingly the default extension of
DFT+U — this work sharpens the question of what the
Hubbard projectors fundamentally are, by revealing that
even the established inter-site correction is, at heart, a
choice of projectors. Determining how projectors might
be chosen optimally is now all the more pressing.

S1.
GLOBAL VERSUS LOCAL CURVATURE
Many-body SIE is the erroneous curvature of the total
energy with respect to the total number of electrons in
the entire system. This assumes that correcting local cur-
vature (i.e. curvature with respect to the occupancy of
the Hubbard site) helps to address global curvature (i.e.
curvature with respect to the total number of electrons in
the entire system) — which is not necessarily the case [1].
Enforcing the linearity condition of the total energy with
respect to the local site occupation can be alternatively
justified in the limit that the on-site electronic interac-
tions are so strong that it is a good approximation to
neglect all other electron-electron interactions, in which
case the rest of the system only interacts with the Hub-
bard site via particle exchange (so that the total energy
should indeed be linear in the site occupation).

S4.
COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS
The illustrative Hubbard projectors and potentials of
NiO presented in Figs. 1 and 2 were obtained from
DFT+U +V calculations of antiferromagnetic NiO per-
formed with Quantum ESPRESSO [5, 6]. NiO was mod-
eled in its rock-salt structure with the experimentally
observed type-II antiferromagnetic ordering (alternat-
ing ferromagnetic (111) planes of opposite spin), using
the corresponding four-atom rhombohedral magnetic cell
(two Ni and two O; conventional cubic lattice param-
eter a = 4.09 ˚A). The calculations were spin-polarized
and used the PBEsol exchange-correlation functional [7],
norm-conserving pseudopotentials from the PseudoDojo
library (version 0.4.1; standard accuracy) [8, 9], wave-
function and charge-density kinetic-energy cutoffs of 70
and 280 Ry, respectively, an 8 × 8 × 8 k-point grid, and
cold smearing [10] of width 0.02 Ry. The Hubbard pro-
jectors were the orthogonalized atomic Ni 3d and O 2p
orbitals (the “ortho-atomic” scheme).
The Hubbard parameters were computed from first
principles using density-functional perturbation theory,
