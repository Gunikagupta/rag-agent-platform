The Label Complexity of Class Conditional Coverage
under Distribution Shift
Weijia Han
Lisha Qu
University of Washington
Abstract
Standard evaluation of many recognition systems contains distribution shift by construction, since
benchmarks place disjoint conditions in the training and test splits. Under such a shift, split conformal
prediction keeps marginal coverage close to the nominal level while per class coverage fails silently. On
a real cross subject skeleton benchmark the marginal coverage stays near ninety percent, the worst action
class is covered about seventy percent of the time, and ten of the sixty classes fall below eighty percent
coverage. Marginal coverage is the wrong safety target in this regime.
This paper characterizes the cost of restoring per class validity. Our first result is an impossibility.
Once the shift acts jointly on the covariates and the labels, the target class conditional score law is
unidentified from source labels and an unlabeled target sample (methods here are label free: they see
no target labels), so no label free method attains per class coverage that is at once valid and efficient.
Our second result makes the cost precise. Per class validity alone needs only a handful of target labels
per class; the per class label count necessary and sufficient for validity together with per class efficiency
grows as the inverse square of the efficiency tolerance and the logarithm of the number of classes, and
our upper and lower bounds match. Our third result addresses the natural shortcut. Within the evaluated
prediction powered inference family, even the most favorable use of the classifier’s own pseudo labels
on an unbounded unlabeled target pool improves the achievable efficiency by at most a small constant
factor on the classes where coverage collapses.
Skeleton action recognition is our real data case study. A per class calibration that uses source
labels alone recovers a substantial share of the per class gap while the shift preserves marginal coverage,
and it stops helping exactly when marginal coverage itself breaks. A spectrum of three real shifts of
increasing severity traces this boundary, and the same collapse and recovery appears on a natural image
corruption benchmark, which places the phenomenon in conditional coverage under shift beyond any
single modality.
1
Introduction
Skeleton action recognition benchmarks build distribution shift into their evaluation protocols by design.
Cross subject and cross view splits train and test on disjoint people and cameras. Split conformal prediction
gives finite sample distribution free coverage under exchangeability [Vovk et al., 2005, Lei et al., 2018].
That guarantee is marginal, and it degrades once calibration and test distributions diverge [Tibshirani et al.,
2019, Barber et al., 2023]. On a real cross subject skeleton benchmark, split conformal prediction keeps
marginal coverage near ninety percent while per class coverage collapses silently: the worst action class is
covered about seventy percent of the time, and ten of the sixty classes fall below eighty percent coverage
(Figure 1). The shift affects classes unevenly, so this collapse stays invisible to any diagnostic that reports
only the marginal number. Marginal coverage is the wrong safety target in this regime.
This paper asks a precise question: what is the label cost of restoring per class validity under distribution
shift? Three theoretical results answer it, and skeleton action recognition serves as the real data case study
that shows the phenomenon and a partial, label free remedy.

Contributions.
1. Impossibility. Under a shift that acts jointly on covariates and labels, the target class conditional score
law is unidentified from labeled source data and an unlabeled target sample. No label free method can
therefore be both valid and efficient per class (Proposition 2).
2. Tight label complexity. Per class validity alone needs only O(1/α) target labels per class. The per
class label count necessary and sufficient for validity and per class efficiency grows as the inverse square
of the efficiency tolerance and the logarithm of the number of classes, and our upper and lower bounds
match (Theorem 1). To our knowledge this is the first matching label complexity characterization for
conformal prediction under distribution shift.
3. Pseudo label ceiling. Within the evaluated prediction powered inference family, even the most favorable
pseudo label estimator, built on an unbounded unlabeled target pool, raises efficiency by at most a small
constant factor on the classes where coverage collapses (Section 5.5). Unlabeled target data offers no
shortcut around this label cost in that family.
4. Real data case study. On skeleton action recognition, a label free per class calibration on the source
domain (label free means it requires no target labels; labeled source calibration data is permitted) recov-
ers a substantial share of the per class gap while marginal coverage holds. A severity spectrum of three
real shifts marks the boundary where this label free recovery stops. The same phenomenon reproduces
on natural images (supplementary material). See Figure 2 and Figure 3.
Scope.
The per class guarantee behind the real data calibration is exact under within class exchangeabil-
ity and only approximate under distribution shift, which bounds how much the label free calibration can
recover. The conformal backbone we evaluate also trails the strongest supervised head [Chen et al., 2021]
(Section 5.6).

Related Work
Conformal prediction and distribution shift.
When the test distribution shifts away from the split CP
exchangeability regime [Vovk et al., 2005, Lei et al., 2018], two strands restore guarantees: weighted con-
formal prediction under covariate shift, which reweights calibration scores by the ratio of the target density
to the source density [Tibshirani et al., 2019], and non exchangeable conformal prediction, which bounds
the coverage gap by a total variation style weight discrepancy [Barber et al., 2023]. Both strands take the
density ratio as input; we estimate it on the manifold of symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices (Sec-
tion 3). Adaptive conformal inference [Gibbs and Cand`es, 2021] targets sequential settings; ours is a batch
setting. The classification scores we compare against are the least ambiguous set valued classifier score
(LAC) [Sadinle et al., 2019], adaptive prediction sets (APS) [Romano et al., 2020], and regularized APS
(RAPS) [Angelopoulos et al., 2021].
Class conditional and Mondrian conformal prediction.
Mondrian conformal prediction [Vovk et al.,
2003, 2005] partitions the calibration data by a “taxonomic” criterion, here the true source class label, and
calibrates a separate quantile per partition. Per class coverage is guaranteed under within class exchange-
ability. Class conditional CP has been studied by L¨ofstr¨om et al. [2015] and Sadinle et al. [2019], and
more recently by Ding et al. [2023]. Our case study instantiates Mondrian conformal prediction on the SPD
feature manifold, using the log Euclidean geometry for per class scoring, and shows it reduces per class
undercoverage on the real NTU RGB+D 60 skeleton benchmark (NTU-60) [Shahroudy et al., 2016] under
cross subject shift, label free.
2
Selective prediction and conformal risk control.
Conformal Risk Control (CRC) generalizes conformal
coverage to any bounded, monotone risk [Angelopoulos et al., 2024]; we instantiate it using a joint accept
and err loss, so the controlled quantity is the probability of accepting a miscovered point. Treating out
of distribution (OOD) detection and selective safety as distinct is motivated by evidence that a high OOD
detection AUROC (area under the ROC curve) can coexist alongside unsafe selective accuracy under severe
shift [Khanal and Zhou, 2026]; we reproduce this phenomenon under a coverage lens and identify a manifold
signal that escapes it.
Geometry of SPD matrices.
SPD descriptors of joint trajectories are a standard action representation,
and SPD manifold networks learn on them directly [Huang and Van Gool, 2017]. The two common metrics
are the affine invariant Riemannian metric, invariant under the full congruence action of the general linear
group [Pennec, 2006, Bhatia, 2007], and the log Euclidean metric, whose geodesic distance is the Frobenius
distance between matrix logarithms and which admits a closed form mean and an isometric tangent space
vectorization [Arsigny et al., 2006]. We use the log Euclidean metric throughout: it gives the closed form
Karcher means our signals consume and an exact distance preserving flattening (Section 4), at the cost of
full affine invariance, a trade off we make explicit.
Conformal prediction on manifolds and prototypes.
Distance to prototype nonconformity scores are
known in flat feature space, including categorical and prototype conformal predictors [Caprio, 2025]; con-
formal inference has also been extended to regression on Riemannian manifolds. Our geodesic to Karcher
score is the SPD manifold instantiation of this idea; matching the literature and our own ablations, the in
distribution efficiency gain of geometry over a flat softmax baseline is real but modest. In the case study, the
load bearing element is label free per class calibration under the benchmark’s own shift, separate from the
geodesic score considered in isolation.
Action recognition: accuracy versus reliability.
Conformal prediction has been applied to human ac-
tion recognition using vision language models, evaluated in distribution and for RGB set size control [Bary
et al., 2025]. Skeleton domain adaptation methods (graph based alignment, contrastive view invariance,
backbones such as the spatial temporal graph convolutional network (ST-GCN) and the channel wise topol-
ogy refinement graph convolutional network (CTR-GCN)) improve target accuracy [Yan et al., 2018, Chen
et al., 2021]; none provide coverage guarantees or per class reliability. Our reliability layer is backbone
agnostic: it consumes descriptors and class means, and it complements these methods without competing
against them. To our knowledge, the case study is the first report of the per class coverage collapse on real
NTU-60 cross subject under an SPD and ST-GCN conformal setup, an empirical instance of the impossi-
bility and label complexity results above that we address in part using label free Mondrian calibration on
the SPD manifold. Datasets used are NTU-60 and Northwestern-UCLA (N-UCLA) [Wang et al., 2014]
(Section 5).
3
Method
Setup.
Each input sequence is summarized by an SPD descriptor, the temporal covariance of joint coordi-
nates and velocities. A source domain supplies labeled descriptors. A target domain supplies only unlabeled
descriptors at deployment, and its distribution can differ from the source.
We study three backbone configurations that jointly vary the SPD representation and its classifier. The
first is a nearest mean geometric classifier: it scores each class through a log Euclidean nearest class mean
rule, and it is the weakest of the three, producing the largest prediction sets. The second is a tangent space
logistic classifier: it flattens each descriptor into a Euclidean tangent space and fits a logistic classifier there,
3
giving moderate discriminative power. The third builds on features from ST-GCN [Yan et al., 2018]: per
frame features from the graph convolutional backbone are summarized into a temporal covariance SPD
descriptor and classified by the same tangent space logistic classifier; this is the strongest configuration
we test (accuracies appear in the supplementary material). The ST-GCN feature configuration is evaluated
under two training regimes of increasing length, detailed in the supplementary material. A gap between the
graph convolutional network’s own classification head and the conformal backbone built from its features
is expected: the SPD framework operates on descriptors extracted from the network, using its own separate
classifier.
Source data is split into a scorer training subset, which fits the backbone classifier, and a calibration sub-
set, which estimates conformal quantiles. All four calibration methods described below share one backbone
and one calibration pool, so their comparison is fair. The geometric classifier and the tangent space classi-
fiers both build on a log Euclidean Karcher mean per class and an isometric log Euclidean vectorization of
the SPD descriptor. Both constructions, alongside the descriptor dimensions, the calibration set sizes, and
the training regimes, are given precisely in the supplementary material.
Nonconformity score.
All four calibration methods score a candidate class by one minus the backbone’s
estimated probability for that class, the LAC nonconformity score [Sadinle et al., 2019]. For the nearest
mean configuration, this probability is a softmax over negative geodesic distances to the class means. For
the tangent space configurations, it is the logistic classifier’s own output. The exact score formulas appear
in the supplementary material.
3.1
Split CP baseline
The split conformal baseline calibrates a single threshold from the source calibration set: the empirical
quantile of the true class scores at the target coverage level. A test point’s prediction set contains every class
whose score falls below this one threshold. This preserves marginal coverage in distribution. Under deploy-
ment shift, marginal coverage can remain near or even above the nominal level while per class coverage
collapses, and only under severe shift does marginal validity itself break (Section 5).
3.2
Source Mondrian (class conditional) calibration
A single global threshold is too tight for classes whose score distribution the shift distorts adversely. Source
Mondrian calibration computes a separate conformal quantile for each class, estimated from the source cali-
bration scores that belong to that class alone. The exact order statistic formula is given in the supplementary
material. A test point’s prediction set then contains every class whose score falls below that class’s own
threshold.
Guarantee and deployment.
The per class thresholds are estimated entirely on the source calibration
set, using source labels only to partition calibration scores by class. No target labels are needed. Under
within class exchangeability, source Mondrian provides per class marginal coverage at least the nominal
level (Proposition 1). Under deployment shift this guarantee holds only approximately (Remark 1), which
is why source Mondrian closes part of the oracle gap and leaves the rest open.
3.3
Weighted conformal calibration
Weighted conformal prediction [Tibshirani et al., 2019, Barber et al., 2023] restores marginal coverage under
covariate shift, given valid density ratio weights, by reweighting calibration scores through an estimated
source to target density ratio in place of the plain empirical quantile; with a joint shift and estimated weights,
4
as here, its use is empirical rather than guaranteed. We use the exact per test point variant of this weighted
quantile, which normalizes the calibration weights separately for every test point; the plain single threshold
variant fails to recover coverage on our controlled synthetic study. Both quantiles are stated precisely in the
supplementary material.
The density ratio is estimated directly from the SPD descriptors: a regularized logistic discriminator
separates source from target descriptors on a held out pool and is applied out of sample. Its regularization,
clipping, and temperature constants are given in the supplementary material. We also evaluate a weighted
class conditional variant, combining the density ratio weights and the per class quantiles of source Mondrian.
This combination inflates prediction sets severely: high variance density ratio weights applied to small per
class subsets drive the per class weighted quantile up.
3.4
Margin abstention (negative control)
We study two geometric per sample abstention signals built from geodesic distances to class means: a
minimum distance signal that flags points far from every class, and a geodesic margin that flags points
close to two competing classes at once. Both signals are defined precisely in the supplementary material.
We threshold the margin signal using Conformal Risk Control [Angelopoulos et al., 2024], which requires a
labeled target calibration pool to set the threshold (Section 4.4). This requirement makes margin abstention a
negative control: it cannot serve as a deployable label free rule. Even given target labels, it fails to reduce per
class undercoverage on the real NTU-60 benchmark, a mechanism detailed in the supplementary material.

5
Experiments
5.1
Per class coverage collapse and recovery on NTU-60 cross subject
Setup.
We use the NTU RGB+D 60 cross subject split [Shahroudy et al., 2016] and an ST-GCN backbone
[Yan et al., 2018]. We fit the backbone on a scorer training subset of the source domain, calibrate conformal
quantiles on a disjoint source pool, and deploy on the target domain. The conformal framework uses a
tangent space logistic classifier on the ST-GCN feature SPD descriptor and the LAC score (Section 3). The
miscoverage level is ten percent, for a nominal coverage of ninety percent; results are the mean over ten
seeds and sixty classes. The split sizes and the backbone accuracies are reported in the supplementary
material. Worst class coverage is noisier across seeds than marginal coverage, since it tracks a single hard
class, and the ranking among methods stays stable.
Collapse.
Split conformal prediction reaches near nominal marginal coverage while a large fraction of
classes fall below eighty percent per class coverage (Figure 1). The worst action class is covered about
seventy percent of the time, against a target label oracle near ninety percent (the oracle calibrates per class
9
conformal quantiles on labeled target data from the deployment distribution, a finite audit whose construc-
tion the supplementary material states; it is a ceiling, not a deployable method). Marginal coverage alone
hides this entirely, since the shift distorts classes unevenly.
Recovery.
The recovery frontier (Figure 2) summarizes the comparison. Source Mondrian, using source
labels alone, closes a substantial share of the oracle gap at a small set size cost, and it does so without
changing marginal coverage: it gives the smallest sets among the methods that materially raise the worst
class. Weighted conformal prediction reaches a somewhat higher worst class, and it pays for that in two
ways at once, a much larger set size and marginal over coverage; the weighted class conditional combination
reaches a similar worst class only by inflating sets further, since high variance density ratio weights on small
per class subsets drive its per class quantiles up. In summary, source Mondrian is the efficient label free
recoverer, the one that keeps sets small and holds marginal coverage on target, while the weighted methods
buy a higher worst class at the price of efficiency. Margin abstention, even given a labeled target pool for
threshold selection, fails to reduce per class undercoverage: its guarantee controls only a joint marginal risk
(supplementary material). Full six method numbers are in the supplementary material.
Robustness.
The pattern holds across backbones. Sweeping the three backbone configurations of Sec-
tion 3, source Mondrian remains the near nominal marginal, label free recoverer at every accuracy level,
and prediction sets tighten as the backbone grows stronger; the sweep varies representation and classifier
together, so it traces a trend without isolating either factor (full numbers in the supplementary material).
The pattern also holds across the coverage level: sweeping the nominal coverage target, the split conformal
worst class gap to nominal widens as the target tightens, while source Mondrian raises the worst class at
every level and tracks the split conformal marginal coverage (full numbers in the supplementary material).
5.2
Cross dataset shift from NTU to N-UCLA, where marginal coverage itself breaks
We test a far harsher shift: we calibrate on NTU-60 and deploy on N-UCLA, a different sensor (Kinect
v1 versus v2), different subjects, and different views. We map NTU’s twenty five joints onto the twenty
shared Kinect v1 joints and restrict both domains to the five shared actions (drop, pick up, throw, sit down,
stand up); skeletons are spine normalized so only pose and motion differ, and scale does not. The tangent
backbone still transfers meaningfully (target top-1 accuracy well above chance; the target oracle recovers
cleanly, per class coverage near ninety percent), so the shift is severe and it is not degenerate.
Finding: the shift is severe enough to break marginal coverage.
Here even marginal coverage collapses
(full six method breakdown in the supplementary material): split conformal prediction reaches only about
half the nominal marginal coverage, producing mostly empty prediction sets, and one class is covered none
of the time. The score distribution shifts globally, so neither the source calibrated global threshold nor the
per class source thresholds of source Mondrian transfer, and none of the evaluated source only, label free
methods recover, consistent with, though not implied by, Proposition 2 once the shift acts on both covariates
and labels. The failure is not attributable to a poorly chosen weight: a geometry aware label free candidate,
which reweights per class quantiles by log Euclidean geodesic proximity to the target class means, also
undercovers. Its weights keep a healthy effective sample size on every class, and even so the reweighted
source quantiles fall well short of every class’s target threshold, since source true class scores concentrate
too low, exactly the support gap the impossibility result rests on. Recovery requires target information:
weighted conformal prediction, label free and using the unlabeled target pool, restores marginal coverage
only by over covering, and it still leaves the worst class below nominal at a large set size cost, and only
the target label oracle attains a clean ceiling on both marginal and worst class coverage. This bounds the
10
case study calibration honestly: source Mondrian’s regime is the one where the shift preserves marginal
coverage, as in the cross subject case; once that breaks, as in the cross sensor case here, unlabeled target
reweighting restores at most the marginal by overcovering, and per class recovery requires target labels.
Marginal coverage itself is thus the regime diagnostic.
5.3
Cross view, and the severity spectrum
Re-partitioning the same NTU-60 samples by camera (train cameras two and three, test camera one) gives
a milder shift than cross subject. Even here, split conformal prediction overcovers marginally and it still
leaves the worst class in an unsafe range, so per class undercoverage persists even when marginal coverage
is above nominal (full per class numbers in the supplementary material). Source Mondrian recovers a
substantial share of the oracle gap label free while keeping marginal coverage on target at the nominal level,
at a modest set size cost; the weighted methods over cover, and the weighted class conditional variant inflates
sets substantially. This mild shift is the left end of the severity spectrum (Figure 3).
The severity spectrum.
Across three real NTU based shifts of increasing severity (Figure 3), source
Mondrian’s label free per class recovery degrades in step, and the failure mode itself changes: marginal
coverage holds under the mild and moderate shifts, and it collapses only under the severe one (Section 5.2).
The severe point is a cross dataset stress test on five shared classes, a different task setting from the two
sixty class NTU shifts, so we read the spectrum qualitatively. The symptoms separating these regimes,
prediction sets collapsing toward empty and the source and target score distributions diverging, are visible
label free, so a deployed system can flag from the unlabeled target pool alone whether label free recovery is
even plausible. Confirming the per class collapse itself requires a labeled audit.
5.4
Controlled synthetic study
A controlled synthetic class conditional SPD shift reproduces the mechanism in isolation and confirms that
margin abstention fails to address class systematic undercoverage even under a labeled target pool CRC
threshold; full details, including the signal dichotomy between the out of distribution signal and the margin
signal, are in the supplementary material.
5.5
Can unlabeled target data substitute for labels? The pseudo label efficiency ceiling
Question.
Theorem 1 proves that unlabeled target data cannot improve the minimax rate of per class
coverage recovery. The classifier under evaluation also emits free pseudo labels on the unlabeled target
pool, so we ask whether the best possible estimator built from those pseudo labels recovers any of the
missing efficiency in practice. We test this using PPI [Angelopoulos et al., 2023], which uses pseudo label
statistics on the unlabeled pool as control variates and a handful of true target labels to debias the per class
quantile estimate. We report the oracle ceiling on its achievable gain: a label efficiency gain computed from
how strongly a two dimensional pseudo label control span explains the naive per class quantile estimator,
evaluated at the oracle tuning parameter, an unbounded unlabeled pool, and the true target quantile. This is
an asymptotic best case benchmark for this control span, and it is not a deployable estimator; finite sample,
tunable estimators should not be expected to improve on it systematically. Every reported number passes an
integrity check: no class is non finite or skipped, and the multivariate gain never falls below the scalar gain,
on both datasets.
Finding: negligible ceiling exactly where it matters.
On both real shifts we test, the median gain over all
classes is modest, and only a small tail reaches three times the baseline (full breakdown in the supplementary
11
material). Restricted to the classes whose split conformal per class coverage collapses below eighty percent,
the ceiling collapses further: the eight collapsing NTU-60 classes cap near a factor of one, and the ninety
seven collapsing CIFAR-100-C classes cap near a factor of one as well, and none reach three times the
baseline on either dataset. The intrinsically hard, most confusable NTU actions are the worst served, each
capping near a factor of one. Gain tracks classifier accuracy closely on both datasets: the shift decorrelates
the pseudo label from the truth on precisely the classes whose coverage collapses, so the signal that control
variate PPI relies on all but vanishes exactly where efficiency is needed.
This is an empirical measurement, and it is not a theorem: it complements Theorem 1, which already
proves unlabeled data cannot improve the minimax rate, by showing that even the best case pseudo la-
bel estimator, using oracle tuning, an infinite unlabeled pool, and the true quantile, fails to extract useful
efficiency where coverage collapses, on two real shifts and two modalities. The ceiling upper bounds de-
ployable estimators built on this pseudo label control span within the asymptotic PPI++ family, and it is not
itself deployable. The untuned, standard deployable form performs worse than the naive audit: it inflates
per class quantile variance and newly breaks per class validity. The full label budget curves and deployable
form results are in the supplementary material.
5.6
Limitations
Backbone.
The SPD conformal backbone, a tangent classifier on the SPD descriptor, lags the GCN head
and stronger supervised baselines such as CTR-GCN [Chen et al., 2021]. Source Mondrian’s per class re-
covery builds on top of this suboptimal starting point, and even the target label oracle’s worst class coverage
reflects an unsolved accuracy reliability trade off. The collapse and recovery pattern is not tied to this back-
bone: a stronger CTR-GCN feature extractor reproduces it, and conformalizing CTR-GCN’s own softmax
head shows that the intrinsically hard NTU classes (reading, writing, type on keyboard) stay undercovered
even at higher accuracy, and source Mondrian recovers them label free (supplementary material). The resid-
ual collapse thus persists across the tested backbones and heads, consistent with a data intrinsic mechanism.
The backbone sweep (supplementary material) also varies representation and classifier together, so recovery
differences cannot be attributed to either alone.
Scope and small classes.
All three real skeleton shifts share NTU as the labeled source. The same col-
lapse and recovery pattern, however, reproduces on a different modality and backbone entirely: CIFAR-100
natural images under a corruption shift, using a CLIP zero shot classifier [Radford et al., 2021] (supple-
mentary material). The phenomenon is thus not skeleton specific, and a broader survey of source datasets
remains future work. The severe regime result is a negative one by design: no source only, label free method
recovers once marginal coverage collapses (Section 5.2). Finally, Mondrian conformal prediction needs at
least a few source calibration points per class; very rare classes yield unreliable per class quantile estimates
and remain the residual failure mode, classes staying below eighty percent even after Mondrian correction.
6
Conclusion
This paper characterizes the label cost of per class conformal coverage under distribution shift. Three results
form the contribution. An impossibility result shows that once a shift acts jointly on covariates and labels,
no label free method can be both valid and efficient per class (Proposition 2). A label complexity theorem
shows that the number of labeled target examples per class needed to close that gap grows as the inverse
square of the efficiency tolerance and the logarithm of the number of classes, and our upper and lower
bounds match (Theorem 1). A pseudo label ceiling measurement shows that within the evaluated prediction
powered inference family, even the most favorable use of unlabeled target data, through the classifier’s
12
own pseudo labels, raises efficiency by at most a small constant factor exactly where coverage collapses
(Section 5.5).
Skeleton action recognition is the real data case study that grounds these results. On a real cross sub-
ject benchmark, marginal coverage stays near ninety percent while the worst action class is covered about
seventy percent of the time and ten of the sixty classes fall below eighty percent coverage. A label free per
class calibration on the source domain, source Mondrian, recovers a substantial share of this gap using only
source labels. It does so precisely in the regime the theory predicts, where marginal coverage itself still
holds.
We are equally clear about the boundary. The per class guarantee behind this calibration is exact under
within class exchangeability and only approximate under distribution shift, which is why the calibration
closes only part of the gap. A severity spectrum of three real shifts traces where the label free recovery
stops: it degrades as the shift grows more severe, and the evaluated label free methods fail once the shift
is severe enough that marginal coverage itself collapses (Figure 3). In that regime, per class recovery in
our experiments requires labeled target information, and unlabeled target data supports only conservative
or assumption dependent marginal corrections, consistent with the impossibility result. We release the
seeded scripts and the synthetic harness so each theoretical claim and each empirical number can be verified
independently.

The untuned estimator harms.
At every labeled budget we test, λ=1 PPI is worse than the naive audit

the lowest ten percent of per class coverages, denoted cvar10) is 0.477 for PPI vs. 0.739 for naive; PPI

intrinsically hard classes of Table S3) at M=300, and still on 15 classes (2 hard) at M=600. The measured
per class quantile variance reduction ratio (median over classes, naive audit variance over PPI variance) is

this (harmful) ratio and classifier accuracy 1−ρ of 0.82. On CIFAR-100-C/CLIP the same pattern holds:

M=4000. Both failures are caught by our validity guard before any downstream use, this is why we do not
recommend λ=1 PPI as a method, and why we instead ask what the best possible tuning could achieve.
The oracle ceiling: is the harm fixable by tuning λ?
The λ=1 harm could in principle be a tuning
artifact rather than a structural limit. We therefore compute the best case directly: the oracle 2-D PPI++
label efficiency gain is gain = 1/(1 −R2), where R2 is the squared multiple correlation of the naive
per class quantile influence function with the 2-D span of pseudo label control variates (the pseudo label
indicator 1{ˆy = c} and the thresholded interaction 1{s(X, c) ≤q⋆
c, ˆy = c}), evaluated at the oracle
optimal λ, an unbounded unlabeled pool, and the true target quantile. This is the standard PPI++ efficiency
identity [Angelopoulos et al., 2023] specialized to a scalar per class quantile target; gain ≥1 always, with
equality when the pseudo label carries no information (R2 →0). Because it assumes oracle tuning, an
infinite unlabeled pool, and the (unknown, in deployment) true quantile, it is an asymptotic best case for this
control span; a finite sample, tunable-λ estimator should not be expected to systematically exceed it, so the
reported ceiling is, if anything, favorable to PPI, making the negative reading below conservative rather than
overstated. We certify every reported number: 0 classes are non finite or skipped, and gainmulti ≥gainscalar
holds for every class on both datasets (Table S5).
Mechanism.
On both datasets, gain tracks classifier accuracy closely (Table S5): the pseudo label is a
more informative control variate exactly where the classifier already agrees with the truth. Shift breaks this
precisely on the classes whose per class coverage collapses, it decorrelates the pseudo label from the ground
truth on those classes, so the 2-D control span the estimator relies on carries almost no signal (R2 →0)
where efficiency is needed most; the harder shifted CIFAR-100-C/CLIP setting has both a higher mean
disagreement (¯ρ=0.734 vs. 0.235) and a uniformly lower ceiling than NTU-60. The intrinsically confusable

Coverage-level (α) sweep
To test whether the per class collapse is an artifact of the single operating point α=0.10, we sweep α ∈

Finding.

coverage regime.

S12
Synthetic Controlled Study: Full Details
All numbers and figures in this appendix are from a controlled synthetic SPD shift. They are mechanism
illustrations only and should not be read as real data evidence.
The synthetic study uses a class conditional SPD generator: K=5 classes, 250 descriptors per class,
d=6, in a graded regime (within class spread = shift strength = 0.3). We fix α=0.10 and report two
disjoint seed ranges (primary 0–9, held out 100–109) as mean±std. No hyperparameter is tuned to the
outcome.
H1: Split CP undercovers (SYNTHETIC)
A source calibrated split CP threshold holds at nominal in distribution (source coverage 0.896/0.907) but
undercovers the shifted target, dropping to 0.735/0.791 (Figure S4, Table S9). The shift is detectable, min
distance AUROC 0.900/0.884, yet detectability does not buy safety: at 50% acceptance, selective cover-
age is only 0.757/0.825, below nominal. This is the “high OOD-AUROC̸ = safe selective classification”
phenomenon [Khanal and Zhou, 2026] reproduced under a coverage lens.
H2: Geodesic score tightens in distribution sets (SYNTHETIC)
For reference: a geodesic nonconformity score s(x, c) = dLE(φ(x), Mc) (different from the LAC score used
in the main results) yields tighter in distribution prediction sets than a Euclidean prototype score: 1.54/1.52
classes vs. 3.83/3.75 at matched ≈0.90 coverage, that is 2.29/2.23 classes smaller (Figure S3, Table S8).
The geodesic score gives sets 0.52 classes larger than LAC (1.00/1.02), which exploits softmax sharpness
on this low temperature backbone, a different mechanism. This result supports geodesic geometry for SPD
but is not load bearing.
H3: Weighted CP restores marginal coverage, at a cost (SYNTHETIC)
Weighted exact CP restores marginal target coverage from 0.735/0.791 to 0.956/0.944 but inflates sets
≈4× (Table S9, Figure S4). The marginal recipe does not recover (0.704/0.729). This illustrates why, on
the real benchmark, weighted CP recovers marginal but not per class coverage efficiently.
